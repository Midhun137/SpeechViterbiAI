Stage 06 • Examination Ready

Faculty Presentation & Viva Reference Guide

1. What is Speech Recognition in an HMM Context? ▾

Speech recognition is the task of transforming continuous acoustic waveforms into discrete phonetic units. Because sound waves are perturbed by speaker accent, microphone noise, and room acoustics, we extract discrete feature tokens (e.g. Low, Medium, High energy frames or MFCC centroids). A Hidden Markov Model represents the underlying phonetic or articulatory vocal states (Silent, Vowel, Consonant) which are unobservable directly from audio.

2. Why is Viterbi Superior to Pointwise Independent State Selection? ▾

Independent State Selection: Considers only the instantaneous emission probability $P(O_t \mid S_j)$ at each isolated frame. It completely ignores temporal transitions between consecutive states ($a_{ij}$). Consequently, it can output physically impossible sequences (e.g., transition between states where $a_{ij} = 0$) and is vulnerable to transient acoustic noise.

Viterbi Algorithm: Evaluates the entire sentence or utterance globally, calculating the single most likely path of hidden states that explains the sequence of observations. It guarantees the maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) trajectory by incorporating transition probabilities that enforce phonetic continuity.

3. What Are the Four Formal Steps of the Viterbi Algorithm? ▾

1. Initialization (t=0): $V_0(j) = \pi_j \cdot b_j(O_0)$ and $\psi_0(j) = 0$.

2. Recursion (t=1..T-1): $V_t(j) = \max_i [V_{t-1}(i) \cdot a_{ij}] \cdot b_j(O_t)$, storing winning predecessor in backpointer $\psi_t(j) = \arg\max_i [V_{t-1}(i) \cdot a_{ij}]$.

3. Termination (t=T-1): $\hat{S}_{T-1} = \arg\max_j V_{T-1}(j)$.

4. Backtracking: Reconstructs the optimal state sequence backwards: $\hat{S}_t = \psi_{t+1}(\hat{S}_{t+1})$ for $t = T-2$ down to $0$.

4. Why Must We Use Logarithmic Probabilities? ▾

Multiplying fractional probabilities ($< 1.0$) across multiple time steps causes numbers to shrink exponentially. For an utterance of even 30 frames, the product can drop below $10^{-50}$, causing floating-point underflow to zero. By transforming probabilities with $\ln(P)$, multiplications become additions ($\ln(A \times B) = \ln A + \ln B$), entirely preventing arithmetic underflow.
